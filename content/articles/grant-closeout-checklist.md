## What closeout actually settles

The period of performance ends on a date; the obligations do not. Closeout is the process that settles them: the final story of what the money did (performance report), the final accounting of where it went (financial report), the cleanup of anything still open (obligations, unspent balances, property), and the filing that makes all of it provable for years afterward. The work can continue beyond service delivery, and its duration depends on the obligations and unresolved questions.

This checklist covers both branches — federal awards under the Uniform Guidance and private grants under their agreements — and assumes you are the recipient. If you make subawards, coordinate their deadlines and review work with your own; the pass-through responsibilities require more than copying this checklist. Confirm the applicable regulatory version, agency implementation and award terms.

## Federal closeout checklist (2 CFR 200.344)

Under [2 CFR 200.344(b)–(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.344), a recipient must submit required final reports and liquidate financial obligations no later than **120 calendar days** after its period of performance ends. A subrecipient has **90 calendar days** after its subaward period ends, or an earlier date agreed with the pass-through entity. The agency or pass-through entity may approve justified extensions; a request alone does not change the deadline.

These are recipient reporting and liquidation deadlines, not a promise that every closeout action ends on day 120. Section 200.344(h) requires the federal agency to make every effort to complete its closeout actions within one year. Record the agency's closeout determination separately from your final submission.

- [ ] **Confirm the exact end date** of the period of performance from the award document, including any approved no-cost extension, and record the applicable reporting and liquidation dates, including any earlier agreed subaward date or authorized extension.
- [ ] **Submit the final financial report** on the form the agency requires, reconciled to the general ledger and the approved budget, with cost share and program income reported where the award requires them (2 CFR 200.328).
- [ ] **Submit the final performance report**: accomplishments compared to the objectives of the award, explanations where goals were not met, and any required cost analysis (2 CFR 200.329).
- [ ] **Liquidate financial obligations within the applicable deadline.** Follow up on invoices, subrecipient bills and payroll before the window ends. Escalate unresolved items and any extension request to the agency or pass-through entity; do not decide eligibility solely from when an invoice arrives.
- [ ] **Refund unobligated balances promptly.** Any funds the agency paid that you are not authorized to retain go back; do not wait to be asked.
- [ ] **Settle property and equipment** purchased with award funds according to the award's property terms — request disposition instructions where required, and record what was kept, sold or returned.
- [ ] **Reconcile cost share and program income** one final time; include each where required and retain supporting records.
- [ ] **File the closeout confirmation** from the agency when it arrives, alongside the submission receipts for each final report.
- [ ] **Assign the retention triggers** under [2 CFR 200.334](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.334). Generally use the final financial report date, with separate rules for renewed awards, property and other categories; check holds and written extensions before deleting anything.

[Section 200.345](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.345) preserves specified post-closeout adjustments, including corrections and final indirect-cost adjustments. This is not permission to continue ordinary program spending: [200.403(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.403) distinguishes administrative closeout costs from other costs and sets timing conditions. Ask the responsible finance lead and awarding agency to resolve uncertain treatment.

Section 200.344(i) requires reporting a recipient's **material failure to comply** in SAM.gov through CPARS, including failures involving final reports. Preserve that condition rather than assuming every delay automatically creates a finding. Keep the outstanding-work record, agency correspondence and any authorized extension.

## Private and foundation closeout checklist

For a private award, read the agreement, authorized amendments and applicable law. Do not assume a federal timetable or a standard 30–90-day deadline. A foundation passing through federal funding may impose federal subaward requirements; confirm the funding relationship before choosing the branch.

- [ ] **Re-read the award letter's closing clauses** before the period ends: final report deadline and format, treatment of unspent funds, publicity or acknowledgment obligations, and any record-keeping terms.
- [ ] **Submit the final narrative and financial report** in the funder's format, with the evidence the agreement promises — attendance records, deliverable files, photographs, budget-versus-actuals.
- [ ] **Resolve unspent funds in writing.** Some funders allow reallocation or an extension on request; others expect a return. Ask before assuming, and file the answer.
- [ ] **Close the relationship deliberately**: confirm receipt of the final report, thank the program officer, and record whether and when a renewal conversation should happen. [Grants.gov's post-award guidance](https://www.grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/post-award-phase) recommends communicating with program officers; for a private award, agree on the funder's own process.
- [ ] **Set a retention schedule.** Use the agreement, applicable law and organizational policy, and check for holds. If the agreement is silent or requirements conflict, obtain qualified advice; a federal three-year period is not a universal private-grant rule.

## Common closeout mistakes

Common review questions include whether staff counted the deadline from the correct period-end date, arranged time to review subrecipient reports, retained submission receipts and distinguished an extension request from an approval. Track unpaid obligations before the liquidation deadline rather than waiting for the final report.

An unspent balance requires a decision based on the award, not a last-minute spending target. The [restricted funds guide](/resources/restricted-funds-management-for-nonprofits) explains how spending and the remaining-work forecast support that discussion. Check the actual refund and extension requirements with the agency or funder.

Retention also continues after closeout. Keep the correct reporting-date or category-specific trigger and any holds with the record inventory; the grant end date is not an automatic destruction date.

## Run closeout from 30 days out

As a planning example, hold a pre-closeout review 30 days before the end date, starting earlier for complex awards: list open deliverables, unspent balances by budget line, outstanding invoices, missing evidence and the owner of each. Put every final report on your [reporting calendar](/resources/grant-reporting-calendar-template) with an internal due date well inside the window, and work the list weekly — the broader [post-award checklist](/resources/post-award-grant-management-checklist) shows where this review sits in the full lifecycle.
