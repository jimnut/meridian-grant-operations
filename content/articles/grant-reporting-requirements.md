## What reports do grant funders require?

Funders require the reports named in the award agreement: commonly financial updates, progress reports and a final accounting. This reference covers the main report families for nonprofit recipients, not every agency's conditions. Sources were checked September 24, 2026; the official eCFR snapshot is current through September 22. Confirm agency implementation and your award's applicable terms under [section 200.110](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.110).

Use the following table as an inventory. The federal timing rules come from [2 CFR 200.328](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.328), [200.329](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.329) and [200.344](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.344). An actual award may specify an earlier due date, an anniversary-based performance report or an approved extension. Foundation schedules below are examples, not universal requirements.

| Report | Who requires it | Cadence or timing to check | Evidence needed | Form or format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interim financial | Federal agency, pass-through entity or private funder | Federal: at least annually, generally no more often than quarterly without specific conditions | Reconciled ledger, approved budget, expenditure support, applicable match and program income | SF-425 for federal reporting as instructed; private funder's worksheet otherwise |
| Final financial | Awarding agency or pass-through entity; private funder if required | Federal recipient: no later than 120 days after performance ends; subrecipient: no later than 90 days, subject to applicable terms | Final expenditures, obligations, cash reconciliation and required adjustments | Final SF-425 or funder-specific financial report |
| Performance or progress | Federal agency, pass-through entity or private funder | Federal: generally annual to quarterly; actual award controls | Results against targets, measurement definitions, variance explanations and source records | Agency format; RPPR for applicable research awards; foundation narrative or portal |
| Closeout package | Agency, pass-through entity or private funder | End of the award; federal reporting deadlines as above | Final financial and performance reports plus other required certifications or property information | Collection of required documents; no universal single closeout form |
| Subrecipient reports | Pass-through entity | Subaward schedule and monitoring needs | Financial and performance reports, supporting records, responses to findings | Pass-through template or portal |
| Audit submission | Federal requirements when applicable; other audits can be separately required | Organization's audit period, not each award end | Audit reporting package and data collection information | Federal Audit Clearinghouse submission for applicable audits |
| Foundation progress or final report | Private foundation | Dates or milestones in the agreement | Budget-to-actual schedule, results, challenges and attachments requested | Foundation-specific template, portal, letter or agreed conversation |

Separate the reporting period from the due date: an October submission can cover July–September activity only. Store both dates. The [multiple-grant workflow](/resources/track-multiple-grants-without-missing-deadlines) and [spreadsheet template](/resources/grant-management-spreadsheet-template) help organize these obligations.

## What are the requirements for reporting on a federal grant?

Direct federal recipients report to the federal agency. A subrecipient reports to its pass-through entity under the subaward. Private foundation grants follow their agreements and applicable law; federal forms and deadlines do not automatically apply.

A state agency can provide either state-only funding or federal funding passed through to a nonprofit. Its name does not establish the funding source. Read the award identification, source of funds and incorporated conditions. For a federal subaward, [2 CFR 200.332](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.332) requires the pass-through entity to include federal award information and relevant requirements, including information needed for its own reporting.

Create separate fields for the original funding agency, the organization you report to and the submission destination. Also record the Federal Award Identification Number and Assistance Listing number where applicable. These identifiers prevent a report for one funding stream being filed under another program with a similar name.

Foundation reporting is less standardized: the agreement may require a narrative, financial schedule or meeting. Clarify the format in writing rather than importing a federal schedule. The [post-award guide](/resources/post-award-grant-management) covers turning an agreement into assigned work; the [federal compliance checklist](/resources/federal-grant-compliance-checklist-2-cfr-200) places reporting among broader obligations.

## What belongs in an interim financial report?

An interim financial report should show financial activity for the specified period using the accounting basis and categories the funder requires. Build it from reconciled accounting records, then explain any difference between the report and the grant budget.

The [SF-425 Federal Financial Report on Grants.gov](https://www.grants.gov/forms/form-items-description/fid/751) includes fields for cash, expenditures, obligations, recipient share, program income and indirect expenses. Which fields you complete depends on the agency's instructions and the reporting purpose. A cash balance is not interchangeable with an expenditure balance.

Check whether a field is cumulative or limited to one reporting interval. For example, the [National Park Service's SF-425 instructions](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/historicpreservationfund/completing-the-sf-425.htm) describe block 10 as cumulative from the performance start through the reporting cutoff. Do not substitute the latest quarter's expenditure for a cumulative total.

Before preparing the form, finance should settle four questions:

- What is the reporting period, and is the report cash or accrual based?
- Which posted transactions belong to this award, including approved corrections?
- Which obligations, match contributions and program income must be reported?
- Who will reconcile the totals and approve the certification?

Keep a mapping between internal ledger accounts and the funder's budget categories. If payroll sits in several internal accounts but appears as one report line, retain the calculation that connects them. Rebuilding that mapping every quarter creates avoidable inconsistencies.

Under current [section 200.328](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.328), annual federal financial reports are due no later than 90 calendar days after the reporting period; quarterly and semiannual reports are due no later than 30 days. Use the actual award deadline or an authorized extension, not a familiar date from another agency.

## How is a final financial report different?

A final financial report closes the financial picture for the award's period of performance. It needs a reconciliation of remaining obligations, final charges and any amounts that must be returned or adjusted.

Before marking an SF-425 final, compare the ledger, previous reports and funder payment records. Investigate differences rather than forcing the new total to match an earlier submission. Keep a note explaining corrections and the basis for any revised report.

Final does not mean every later adjustment becomes impossible. [Section 200.344](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.344) requires a final financial report even when the recipient's final indirect cost rate is not available, followed by a revised final report when applicable rates are finalized. Ask the agency or pass-through entity how to handle that circumstance; do not hold the entire submission indefinitely.

The closeout period also does not create an unrestricted extra spending period. Distinguish settling permitted obligations from starting new program activity. Coordinate unusual late charges, rate adjustments or refunds with finance and the award contact.

## What should a performance or progress report show?

A performance report should compare actual results with the award's agreed goals, explain material differences and connect activity to the resources used. It should allow a reader to distinguish completed work from planned work.

Under [section 200.329](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.329), federal performance reporting relates financial information and accomplishments to award objectives. Applicable reports include explanations of unmet goals and cost overruns. Significant developments may need notification between scheduled reports; a recurring deadline is not permission to wait when a serious problem emerges.

Use a small evidence table behind the narrative: indicator, target, actual, reporting period, source and reviewer. For a hypothetical youth program, “120 workshop attendances” and “120 distinct participants” are different measures. The narrative should use the measure the award specifies and explain its counting method.

Research awards may use a specialized structure. [NIH's RPPR guidance](https://grants.nih.gov/grants-process/post-award-monitoring-and-reporting/reporting-requirements/research-performance-progress-report-rppr) distinguishes annual, interim and final Research Performance Progress Reports. Its interim RPPR concerns a Type 2 renewal, not a generic midyear update. NIH submissions use eRA Commons; RPPR is not required for every federal nonprofit award.

## What is included in a grant closeout package?

A closeout package is the set of final documents the award requires. It can include the final financial report, final performance report and additional property, invention or other certifications when applicable.

For example, the [EPA's official forms directory](https://www.epa.gov/grants/epa-applicant-and-recipient-forms) identifies SF-425 for financial reporting and SF-428 for tangible personal property. That does not mean every award requires every listed form. Check whether your project acquired property and which instructions the agency attached to the award.

Make closeout a checklist of separate deliverables. Each needs an owner, approver, due date, evidence list and submission receipt. “Closeout complete” is too broad to identify that finance has finished but the program narrative still lacks approval.

Use the [grant closeout checklist](/resources/grant-closeout-checklist) for the operational handoff. It separates the work your organization must finish from the agency's administrative decision to close the award.

## What reports should a subrecipient provide?

A subrecipient should provide the financial and performance reports required by its subaward, together with the support and corrective-action information the pass-through entity is entitled to request. The pass-through entity must review those reports as part of monitoring under [section 200.332](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.332).

If your nonprofit makes subawards, work backward from your own report date. Agree submission dates that leave time to review the material and resolve discrepancies. Record acceptance and follow-up questions, not only the date a file arrived.

If your nonprofit receives a subaward, use the dates in that agreement. The direct recipient's 120-day final-report deadline is not automatically your deadline. Section 200.344 gives subrecipients 90 days or an earlier agreed date, subject to approved extensions. A documented reporting schedule protects both sides from incompatible assumptions.

## When is an audit submission required?

A federal audit submission is separate from routine award reporting. Under [2 CFR 200.501](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.501), a non-Federal entity subject to Subpart F generally needs a Single Audit when federal awards expended during its fiscal year total $1 million or more; eligible program-specific audits are an alternative. This is an entity-wide expenditure test, not an award-value or cash-receipt test. Subpart F does not apply to for-profit organizations.

The [Federal Audit Clearinghouse's eligibility guidance](https://www.fac.gov/_collections/walkthrough/04-eligibility/) applies the $1 million threshold to fiscal years starting on or after October 1, 2024. For earlier-starting fiscal years, the threshold is $750,000. Check the audit period, not just the submission year.

Under [section 200.512](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.512), the audit, data collection form and reporting package are generally due within 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's reports or nine months after the audit period ends, whichever is earlier. The section addresses extensions and weekend or federal-holiday treatment.

Work with your auditor: individual grant reports do not replace the audit package. Below the threshold, other funder, state or organizational audit requirements may still apply, and federal records remain subject to appropriate review.

## What dates follow an award ending September 30?

For an illustrative award ending September 30, 2026, 30 days later is October 30, 90 days later is December 29 and 120 days later is January 28, 2027. The following example assumes the current general rules apply, no approved extension and no earlier agreement.

| Date | What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| September 30, 2026 | Period of performance ends | Establishes the final program cutoff |
| October 30, 2026 | Any separately required quarterly report for the quarter ending September 30 | A final report does not automatically cancel another required report |
| December 29, 2026 | Subrecipient final reports and liquidation; any applicable 90-day annual report | Distinguish subaward deadlines from direct-recipient deadlines |
| January 28, 2027 | Direct recipient final reports and liquidation | 120 calendar days after the performance end |
| June 30, 2027 | Nine-month audit deadline if the organization's audit period also ended September 30, 2026 | An earlier auditor-report receipt date can bring the submission forward |

Do not add every row mechanically. Confirm which interim reports remain required in the final cycle, and add internal review dates before the external deadlines. If the auditor's reports arrive on May 15, 2027, the illustrative 30-day audit date is June 14, earlier than June 30.

## What is grant reporting software?

Grant reporting software organizes obligations, budget information, supporting evidence and preparation status so the team can assemble a report and show what remains unresolved. Evaluate it separately from the funder's submission portal and your accounting system.

## How do you keep a report ready for review after submission?

Preserve the exact submitted version, attachments, approval and receipt as a single identifiable record. A draft in a shared folder does not establish what the funder received.

Start with the [reporting calendar template](/resources/grant-reporting-calendar-template), and maintain the source trail described in the [audit-ready grant file guide](/resources/audit-ready-grant-file). Record when the agency requests corrections and retain the replacement submission beside the original.

Federal records generally must be retained for three years from submission of the final financial report under [section 200.334](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.334). Renewed awards and some record categories have different triggers. Litigation, claims or audits started before expiry require retention through resolution and final action; written extensions also matter. The audit package has a separate three-year FAC-submission trigger under [section 200.512(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.512). Confirm the applicable trigger and holds with your funder, finance lead or qualified adviser before disposing of records.
