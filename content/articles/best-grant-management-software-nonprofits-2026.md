## What are the best grant management systems for nonprofit organizations?

Build your shortlist around the work your team must complete. A grants manager researching funders needs different records from a foundation reviewing applications, or a finance lead reconciling restricted spending. Some platforms cover more than one job, so the tables below organize buying decisions rather than impose hard boundaries on products.

This comparison is written by GrantConsole, which sells recipient-side software. Product descriptions and public prices were checked on September 21, 2026. Best-fit recommendations are editorial judgments based on documented scope; we have not run a comparative usability or implementation test.

Use these three questions to choose where to start:

1. **Do you need to find and apply for funding?** Start with grantseeker tools. Test prospect relevance and the application workflow.
2. **Do you distribute funding to other organizations or people?** Start with grantmaker tools. Test intake, review, decisions, payments, and follow-up.
3. **Do you already hold awards and need to deliver against them?** Start with recipient operations and accounting. Test obligations, budget periods, supporting evidence, and reporting.

A nonprofit that both receives money and makes subawards should evaluate both sides. Buying only for incoming applications can leave its own upstream reporting work unresolved. Buying only an internal deadline tracker can leave application review and subrecipient administration unresolved.

For recipient responsibilities, start with the [post-award grant management guide](/resources/post-award-grant-management). The [post-award checklist](/resources/post-award-grant-management-checklist) turns those responsibilities into tasks you can take into a demonstration.

GrantHub belongs in the conversation because people still encounter the name in older comparisons. Submittable belongs because applicants may use a funder's portal, although buying its grantmaking system is a different decision.

## Where do donor CRMs belong in a grant software comparison?

Donor CRMs belong when fundraising relationships are part of the problem. They should be evaluated separately from claims about post-award finance and compliance. [Bloomerang](https://bloomerang.com/), [DonorPerfect](https://www.donorperfect.com/), and [Neon CRM](https://neonone.com/solutions/neon-crm-overview/) describe fundraising and relationship-management products. Neon also explicitly documents grant-award, funder-relationship and reporting-deadline tracking. Test those capabilities before assuming you need another tool. A grants team may benefit from shared funder contacts while retaining a separate financial workflow.

[Salesforce's nonprofit offering](https://www.salesforce.com/nonprofit/) covers a wider platform, including grantmaking, fundraising, programs and outcomes. Its name alone does not identify a configured grant-management workflow. Evaluate the specific product, implementation, permissions, and ongoing administration being proposed. The same discipline applies to Blackbaud: a recommendation for Grantmaking is not automatically a recommendation for its financial or fundraising products.

Avoid changing your CRM simply because a comparison lists it beside a grant tracker. First identify the missing record or handoff. If development owns the funder relationship and finance owns expenditure data, the practical question is how the grants lead sees both without reconciling conflicting versions.

## How do you choose the best grant management software for your team?

Use one representative award scenario and one representative user from grants, finance, and programs. Keep demonstration data fictional or appropriately redacted until your organization has approved the vendor's handling of its information. Give each shortlisted vendor the same demonstration tasks. Include a person who only needs to review records, because leadership and external reviewers may require different access from editors.

Ask the team to perform these steps:

1. Enter an award with its own budget period and two different reporting dates.
2. Assign a deliverable and its reviewer, then change the owner.
3. Attach the required evidence and identify what is still missing.
4. Compare approved budget, actual spending, and known commitments.
5. Record a revision without losing the original instruction or approval.
6. Export a usable grant file, including access to the underlying documents.

Record what required configuration, another product, staff work, or a higher plan. Do not award points simply because a feature exists somewhere in a vendor's portfolio. Your comparison should describe the package the organization can buy and maintain.

### Compare the first-year commitment, not just the monthly equivalent

Request the exact plan, billing currency, payment schedule and included capacity in writing. A monthly equivalent for a prepaid annual plan is not the same cash commitment as a monthly subscription. Separate the subscription from implementation, migration, training, integrations and staff administration. Record renewal pricing and cancellation/export terms separately.

For a quote without a public number, do not enter zero in your comparison. Mark it pending and describe what the vendor needs to price: active awards, annual disbursement volume, applicants, reviewer seats, entities, storage or required modules. Give each vendor the same assumptions. Ask which changes trigger a higher tier and whether the quote includes the configuration used in its demonstration.

**Hypothetical cost example, not vendor pricing or a customer result:** suppose one proposal requires USD 2,400 in annual subscription fees and USD 1,000 in setup fees. Your planning assumption is 20 onboarding hours valued at USD 40 each. That produces USD 3,400 in external first-year charges and USD 800 in allocated staff time, or USD 4,200 combined, before taxes or other costs. A second proposal of USD 3,600 with no setup charge and ten onboarding hours at the same assumed rate totals USD 4,000 on that basis.

Those assumptions do not prove the second product is better or that either saves money. Replace them with quoted charges and your own staffing estimate. Salaried staff time is a capacity cost, not necessarily an additional cash payment. Repeat the calculation for renewal, when setup may disappear but subscription rates, grant volume or administration needs may change.

### Test the handoff and the exit

A polished dashboard can conceal work between systems. Ask the finance user to correct one expense after the grants lead has already viewed the report. Does the correction arrive automatically, require an import or require re-entry? Who notices a failed update? Record the answer for the exact proposed integration or manual process, not the vendor's broad feature label.

Next, change a reporting deadline in the fictional award. Have a colleague who missed the demonstration identify the current date, its source and the person responsible. A warning without a reason, or a new date without the underlying amendment, leaves the team dependent on someone else's memory. This is an acceptance test to perform, not a claim that every product implements the same history model.

Finally, open an exported award outside the platform. Check dates, amounts, stable identifiers, document access and links between evidence and obligations. Ask what you can export before cancellation, what remains accessible afterward and what requires additional fees. Have your organization review the actual contract and security terms before uploading confidential grant or participant data.

The [grantee's software buying guide](/resources/choosing-grant-management-software-grantee) expands these tests into ten evaluation criteria. Use it after choosing the relevant table here, so the shortlist and the detailed demonstration solve the same problem.

For a small nonprofit, a controlled spreadsheet can remain a sensible decision. Keep it while responsibilities, evidence, and budget reconciliation are dependable. Revisit software when staff must repeatedly reconstruct the same answer, or when an absence reveals that only one person understands the records.
