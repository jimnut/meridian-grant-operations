## Download the template

Download the CSV here: **[grant-reporting-calendar-template.csv](/templates/grant-reporting-calendar-template.csv)**. It is a plain comma-separated file with ten columns and seven fictional example rows covering private and federal-style awards. Each Notes cell labels the example. Replace all organizations, people, dates and requirements before use; the sample schedule is not an instruction for an actual award. The download is one CSV, which you can open in either Excel or Google Sheets.

The columns, and why each exists:

| Column | What goes in it | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funder | The organization the report goes to | Reports cluster by funder relationship, not by program |
| Grant | The award the report belongs to | One organization can hold several awards from one funder |
| Award period | Start and end dates of the period of performance | Provides context for period-based deadlines |
| Report type | Financial, performance, mid-term, final, renewal | Different types need different preparers and reviewers |
| Due date | The funder's deadline | The date that matters is the funder's, not your internal one |
| Owner | One accountable person, with a backup in your procedure | Makes follow-up responsibility explicit |
| Evidence required | What must exist before the report can be written | Identifies records to collect and review |
| Submitted date | When it actually went out | Proof of on-time submission is audit material |
| Status | Planned, drafting, submitted | The portfolio view: what is moving and what is stuck |
| Notes | Deadline rule, portal, confirmation reference | Where the institutional memory lives |

## Open it in Google Sheets or Excel

**Google Sheets:** create a blank sheet, then File → Import → Upload, choose the CSV and "Replace spreadsheet". Format the Due date column as a date, then add a filter view sorted by due date ascending — that one view is the calendar.

**Excel:** open the file directly, then save as .xlsx so filters and formatting persist. Use Format as Table so new rows inherit the layout.

In either tool, add conditional formatting on the Due date column — highlight anything inside 30 days — and freeze the header row. Add an internal review date or an evidence-link column if your team needs it, and keep one approved working copy. CSV does not preserve spreadsheet formatting, formulas or validation; save those changes in the native workbook or sheet.

## Fill it in: one row per required report

Work grant by grant with the award document open, and enter every report the award actually requires — not the cadence you assume. The [post-award grant management checklist](/resources/post-award-grant-management-checklist) covers this setup stage in full.

For **federal awards**, read the award, amendments and applicable rules before entering a date. [2 CFR 200.328](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.328) and [200.329](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.329) generally require reporting at least annually and no more frequently than quarterly unless specific conditions apply. Ordinary quarterly or semiannual reports have an outside limit of 30 calendar days after the reporting period; annual reports generally have 90 days. Performance-report rules also allow anniversary-based annual dates and certain exceptions, including construction reporting and waivers. An outside limit is not necessarily the deadline in your award.

For **final reports**, [2 CFR 200.344](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.344) distinguishes recipients, with a 120-calendar-day limit after their period ends, from subrecipients, with 90 days after the subaward period ends or an earlier date agreed with the pass-through entity. The agency or pass-through entity may approve justified extensions. Record the actual due date and the written authorization separately from any pending request. Enter financial and performance reports as separate rows when they are separate submissions; the [grant closeout checklist](/resources/grant-closeout-checklist) covers those actions.

For **foundation and corporate grants**, use the agreement's reporting dates and any authorized changes. Do not copy the federal timetable or the CSV examples into a private award. Keep the source requirement or its location in Notes. [Grants.gov's communication guidance](https://www.grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/post-award-phase) recommends clarifying terms with grant and program officers before problems arise; the same habit is useful when a private agreement is unclear.

Two entry habits pay for themselves. First, give every row exactly one owner — a person, not a team. Second, fill Evidence required at entry time, when the award terms are in front of you; it turns each future report from a writing problem into a collection problem, which is easier to schedule.

## The rhythm that makes a calendar work

Choose a review frequency that fits your obligations. This is a suggested starting routine, not a funder requirement:

1. **Weekly, 15 minutes:** filter to everything due inside 30 days. For each row, confirm the owner knows, the evidence is arriving, and the internal review date is set — consider an internal review date two weeks before the funder's date, adjusting the lead time to the report's complexity.
2. **Monthly, with finance:** scan the whole calendar. Mark submitted reports with their submitted date and confirmation reference, re-check upcoming quarters, and add rows for any new award or amendment.
3. **After every submission:** record the date and file the confirmation. A status or date in the sheet is not a substitute for the submission receipt.

## What this CSV does and does not do

If you need award records, grant-period budgets and evidence links alongside the calendar, the [free five-tab grant management workbook](/resources/grant-management-spreadsheet-template) connects those records and includes clearly labeled examples.

This CSV supplies a starting structure, not an automated workflow:

- **Reminders need a separate process.** The file does not send owner alerts. Use scheduled reviews or an approved reminder tool, with backup coverage.
- **Evidence needs a retrievable location.** The default Evidence required field describes what to collect. You can put links in Notes or add a dedicated link column after import; check file permissions and review the documents themselves.
- **Budgets need reconciliation.** The calendar has no spending calculations. Maintain the financial records separately or use a linked workbook, and reconcile them to accounting on an agreed schedule.

Use a shared working copy and define who can edit it. Emailing copies can create conflicting versions unless the team controls which one is authoritative.

## When to move beyond the sheet

Keep the spreadsheet while the team can reliably assign work, retrieve evidence, reconcile budgets and review deadlines. Complexity and handoffs matter more than a fixed number of awards.
