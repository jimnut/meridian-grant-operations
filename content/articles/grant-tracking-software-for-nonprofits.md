## What grant tracking software is for

Grant tracking software manages the money you have already won. The moment an award letter arrives, a nonprofit inherits a set of dated, restricted, evidence-hungry obligations: reports on the funder's clock, deliverables with proof requirements, a budget that may only buy certain things within certain dates, and a closeout with a deadline attached. Tracking software keeps all of that on one record per grant and watches it continuously, so the team learns about a problem when it is still cheap to fix.

That definition excludes two neighbors it is often confused with. It is not grant *discovery* — finding funders is a different job done before the award. And it is not a fundraising CRM — managing donor relationships is about people, not obligations. GrantConsole, the product behind this site, is post-award grant management software for nonprofit grant recipients, and this page describes the category through the workflows it actually covers.

## What a recipient team has to track

Across a portfolio of 5–25 grants, the tracking load is consistent:

- **Deadlines and deliverables.** Financial reports, performance reports, mid-terms, finals, site visits — each with a funder due date and an internal lead time. Federal awards set the cadence in regulation: quarterly or semiannual reports are due within 30 calendar days of the period and annual reports within 90 (2 CFR 200.328), and everything closes out within 120 days of the period's end (2 CFR 200.344).
- **Owners.** Every obligation needs exactly one named person. Most missed deadlines were known dates that belonged to nobody.
- **Renewal windows.** The reapplication conversation has its own clock, usually opening about 90 days before the period ends.
- **Restricted budgets.** Spending measured against the *grant period*, not the fiscal year — a grant 58% spent at 42% elapsed is running hot no matter what month it is.
- **Evidence.** The attendance sheets, invoices, timesheets and deliverable files that prove the work, collected as it happens rather than the week the report is due.
- **Risk across the portfolio.** The one question leadership actually asks — "are we on top of our grants?" — answered per grant, with reasons.

A spreadsheet can hold the first three columns of this list; our [reporting calendar template](/resources/grant-reporting-calendar-template) is exactly that and is the right starting point. The last three are where static sheets give out.

## How GrantConsole tracks each layer

**Deadlines, deliverables and renewals.** Every grant carries its period dates, deliverables and renewal window, each deliverable with a due date, a type and a status. Deadlines resolve in your workspace's configured timezone, and the calendar view runs on the same records the warnings do — there is one source of dates, not a sheet and a separate reminder system.

**Owners.** Grants have a named internal owner, and a grant with no owner is itself a tracked condition — one of the explainable warnings, because unowned grants drift.

**Restricted budgets.** Each grant's budget is planned and spent by line, stored as integer cents, and measured against the elapsed grant period continuously. When spending runs more than 15 points ahead of the clock — or more than 15 behind — the grant is flagged with the calculation stated in plain numbers. The method is the one described in our [restricted funds guide](/resources/restricted-funds-management-for-nonprofits); the software just refuses to forget it between monthly meetings.

**Evidence.** Evidence attaches to the deliverable that requires it, with required and attached counts per deliverable. A report due in ten days with two of five evidence items attached is visible as exactly that — which is the difference between a collection task this week and a scramble next week.

**Explainable risk, not a score.** GrantConsole evaluates active grants against eleven explainable rules — a deliverable past due; an evidence gap inside the final 14 days before a report; spending more than 15 points ahead of, or behind, the elapsed period; a closeout window inside 30 days with work still open; a grant period ended with deliverables still open; overdue tasks; evidence gaps on reports due within 30 days; a renewal window with nothing scheduled; an application due within 14 days; no internal owner assigned. Every warning states which obligation, date, record or owner triggered it, so a person can check the claim instead of trusting a number.

**Reports out.** Reporting packets assemble a grant's full record — period, budget lines with planned and spent totals, the evidence checklist with each document and who uploaded it, tasks, open risks and the activity history — and the portfolio and packet views export to CSV for whatever your finance stack does next.

**Roles and history.** Access is role-based — owner, manager, member, viewer — enforced by the server, and activity history records who changed what, which is most of what an internal reviewer or auditor wants from a system of record.

## Spreadsheet or software: an honest comparison

| Need | Well-kept spreadsheet | GrantConsole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold deadlines and owners | Yes, with discipline | Yes, on the grant record |
| Alert the owner as a date approaches | No — someone must look | Yes — warnings surface per grant |
| Link evidence to the deliverable it proves | No — files live elsewhere | Yes — attached with required counts |
| Show burn vs. elapsed period continuously | Formula per row, updated by hand | Computed continuously, flagged at ±15 points |
| Explain why a grant is at risk | The person who noticed explains | Each warning names its rule and records |
| Survive the maintainer leaving | Rarely | Roles and history stay with the workspace |
| Cost of adoption | Nearly zero | A real setup: grants, budgets, deliverables entered |

Below about five grants, the spreadsheet column wins on simplicity — start with the [template](/resources/grant-reporting-calendar-template) and the [post-award checklist](/resources/post-award-grant-management-checklist). Past that, the middle rows are where deadlines actually get missed, and they are the rows a sheet cannot fill.

## What GrantConsole does not do

It does not find grants, manage donors, or write applications. It is not accounting software: the general ledger, allowability judgments and audit preparation remain with your finance team and their tools. And it does not interpret awards — it tracks the obligations you enter and makes them hard to miss, which is a different thing from deciding what the award requires. The team's judgment, the award document and the regulations stay in charge; details of how the application is secured — password hashing, throttled sign-in, server-enforced roles, validated uploads — are documented plainly on the [security page](/security).
