“Customer Personal Data” means personal data the Provider processes on the Customer's behalf in providing GrantConsole, as described in Schedule 1. “Data Protection Law” means applicable UK data-protection law, including the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 as amended. Terms such as controller, processor, personal data breach and processing have their statutory meanings.

### 1. Roles, scope and instructions

The Customer is controller and the Provider is processor for Customer Personal Data. The Customer determines its lawful purposes, legal bases, notices, data subjects and retention instructions. The Provider will process Customer Personal Data only to deliver the agreed service and on documented Customer instructions, including for international transfers. Instructions consist of this agreement, the accepted schedules, authorised use of service functions and subsequent written instructions from an authorised Customer contact.

The Provider will promptly tell the Customer if an instruction appears to infringe Data Protection Law and pause the affected instruction while seeking clarification. Where domestic law requires other processing, the Provider will inform the Customer of that legal requirement before processing unless legally prohibited on important public-interest grounds. The Provider will not sell Customer Personal Data, use it for advertising or model training, or determine unrelated purposes for it.

The Provider acts separately as controller for its own supplier relationship, customer account administration, billing and public website activity where it determines those purposes; those activities are described in its privacy notice and must not be used to expand the purposes for Customer Personal Data.

### 2. Confidentiality and security

The Provider will limit access to authorised people with a service need and a contractual or statutory duty of confidentiality. It will maintain technical and organisational measures appropriate to the processing risks under Article 32, including the accepted Schedule 2 controls, and regularly assess their effectiveness. It will not materially reduce agreed protections during the service term without agreement to an appropriate replacement.

### 3. Subprocessors

The Customer gives general written authorisation only for the subprocessors identified as approved in the completed supplier schedule attached at acceptance. The Provider will give written notice of proposed additions or replacements, with a meaningful opportunity to object before they begin the relevant processing. Its target notice is 30 days. If a supplier gives a shorter notice, the Provider must obtain specific Customer authorisation or prevent that supplier from processing the affected Customer Personal Data until an acceptable arrangement is reached.

The parties will discuss a substantiated data-protection objection and a reasonable alternative. If unresolved, the Customer may end the affected service and obtain return or deletion under clause 7 before the change. The Provider will impose equivalent applicable data-protection obligations on each subprocessor in a binding written contract, exercise due diligence, and remain responsible to the Customer for its subprocessor's performance of those obligations.

### 4. International processing and access

The Provider will disclose agreed hosting locations, remote-access countries and relevant onward processing in the attached supplier schedule. It will not promise UK-only processing where the agreed schedule permits other locations. Before making a restricted transfer it initiates, it will obtain documented Customer instructions and put in place a valid applicable mechanism, complete the required data-protection test / transfer risk assessment where applicable, and implement supplementary measures where needed. This clause does not itself incorporate an IDTA, UK Addendum or other transfer instrument.

### 5. Rights and regulatory assistance

Taking account of the processing and information available, the Provider will assist the Customer through appropriate technical and organisational measures with individual-rights requests, security, breach assessment and notifications, impact assessments and prior consultation with the regulator. It will promptly forward requests concerning Customer Personal Data and not answer substantively except on Customer instructions or as legally required. It will provide relevant records and assistance in time for the Customer's applicable statutory deadline. Charges for exceptional work must be agreed in advance and must not prevent mandatory assistance.

### 6. Personal data breaches

The Provider will notify the Customer **without undue delay after becoming aware** of a personal data breach affecting Customer Personal Data. It will not wait for a completed investigation. Notification will include known circumstances, affected data and people, approximate numbers where possible, likely consequences, containment/remediation and a contact for updates. Missing information will follow without undue further delay. It will preserve appropriate evidence, record actions and cooperate with the Customer. The Customer decides its controller notifications; the Provider fulfils any separate legal duty it has. A 72-hour controller reporting window is not permission for the Provider to delay notifying the Customer.

### 7. Return, deletion and service ending

At the Customer's choice on service ending, the Provider will return Customer Personal Data in agreed usable formats, then delete it and existing copies, or delete without return, unless applicable law requires storage. The parties will complete the operational deadlines in Schedule 3 before acceptance. Export must include uploaded evidence as well as structured records; a CSV alone is not the full data return.

Backup copies awaiting expiry will be isolated from ordinary use. Any necessary restore will reapply recorded erasure instructions before restored data is returned to service. Lawfully retained data will be identified, restricted to its retention purpose and deleted when the legal requirement ends. Confidentiality and protection obligations continue until final deletion. The Provider will provide a written completion record, including exceptions and the latest backup expiry date, on request.

### 8. Accountability, audit and priority

The Provider will make available information necessary to demonstrate its compliance with these processor obligations and allow and contribute to Customer or appointed independent-auditor audits and inspections. Reasonable notice, confidentiality and proportionate scope may protect other customers without defeating these rights; urgent incidents, credible concerns and regulator requirements may need expedited access. The Provider will inform the Customer immediately if an audit-related instruction appears unlawful.

This DPA prevails over conflicting service terms about processing Customer Personal Data; a mandatory transfer instrument prevails within its scope. Service-contract liability provisions apply only insofar as they do not exclude mandatory statutory responsibilities or data-subject rights. ## Schedule 1 — Processing particulars

| Item | Agreed scope |
| --- | --- |
| Subject matter | Hosted grant operations workspace, associated storage, exports and support |
| Nature | Receive, organise, store, retrieve, amend, transmit authorised notices, export, back up and erase |
| Purposes | Customer-directed grant administration, deadlines, budgets, reporting, team collaboration and service security |
| Duration | Service term plus the completed Schedule 3 return/deletion periods and any specifically identified mandatory legal retention |
| Data subjects | Customer staff, trustees, volunteers, professional funder contacts and other adults whose ordinary work details the Customer lawfully enters |
| Data types | Names, work contact details, roles, author/activity metadata, grant and funder correspondence, notes, uploaded evidence and associated identifiers where personal data occurs |
| Excluded by default | Identifiable beneficiary/client case files; patient/health data; safeguarding material; children's information; special-category or criminal-offence data; identity documents; payment-card details |
| High-risk data exception | No exception is authorised by this draft. It requires a separate written scope, risk/DPIA review, appropriate controls and contractual update before upload |
| Customer obligations and rights | Lawful collection and instructions; minimisation; notices and legal bases; authorised users; instructions for access, correction, export and deletion; assistance, information and audit rights under clauses 5–8 |

Use synthetic, redacted or sufficiently aggregated grant evidence wherever possible. A charity's service area is not authorisation to upload beneficiaries' sensitive records.

## Schedule 2 — Security measures for acceptance

Attach the completed readiness checklist and identify the deployed version/date. The following require verification, not a signature on an unsupported assertion:

| Area | Proposed accepted measure and evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Authentication | Hashed passwords, protected sessions, throttling, password-reset controls; verify deployed 2FA enrolment, challenges, recovery and session invalidation before listing 2FA as available |
| Tenant isolation | Server-enforced workspace boundaries and roles; record cross-tenant negative tests and authorised export/download checks |
| Support access | Named authorised operator, confidentiality duty, approved access country, written customer permission and limited-purpose access record; emergency exception documented separately |
| Transport and storage | HTTPS; confirm production cookie/security headers; Render persistent-disk encryption is provider documented, not evidence of separate app-level encryption |
| Backups and recovery | Identify actual source/destination, encryption and key custody, schedule, alert owner, retention and successful isolated restore in Schedule 3; do not substitute same-disk copies for independent disaster recovery |
| Change and incident control | Reviewed changes, proportionate tests, vulnerability response and the incident/rights procedures in the operating safeguards document, once adopted |
